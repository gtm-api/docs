description: "A workspace buys email servers, connects its mailboxes to them, and sends, syncs and keeps a stop-list through the app, the REST API and the MCP server."

Email runs on email servers your workspace buys. Each server is a deployment of its own with its own
database: the mailboxes connected to it, their mirrored inboxes, scheduled sends, the stop-list and
the delivery records live on that server. A workspace can own several.

## Buying a server

Open **Email Accounts** in the app and add a server: a name and a size. The price is per server, per
month:

| Size | Mailboxes | Price |
|---|---|---|
| Starter | up to 100 | $59 |
| Growth | up to 500 | $119 |
| Agency | up to 2,000 | $249 |
| Dedicated | up to 10,000 | $499 |

The dialog says what the purchase costs before you confirm it. A server is charged when it is bought,
prorated to the current period of your subscription, and is ready a few minutes later; the screen
shows its setup steps while it is built. A workspace with no paid plan buys its first server on a
subscription of its own, and a plan bought later goes on that same subscription.

Once it runs you can rename a server, change its size, move mailboxes to another server and delete it
when it holds no mailbox. Deleting the only server of a subscription leaves the subscription and its
paid seat as they are: the next server of the same size takes that seat with no new charge.

## When a server is not paid for

A server whose subscription is past due beyond its grace or paused, or that is one more than the subscription
pays for, is paused: its mailboxes do not sync and nothing is sent from them. An email to the workspace's owner
then names the moment it is deleted with every mailbox on it, seven days ahead; a deleted server cannot be
restored. Two more emails follow, three days before that moment and the day before, each naming it in UTC, and one
more once the server is gone. Paid for again before it, the server comes back with everything on it. Over the API,
a paused server's `destroy_at` says when, and the webhook events `email-boxes.deletion-warning` (each step, with
`destroy_at`) and `email-boxes.destroyed` (with the cause and the mailboxes that went with it) say it to your own
systems. A plan change that moves a server to a new machine sends `email-boxes.replaced` with the new and the old
server.

## Connecting mailboxes

- Google and Microsoft mailboxes connect through their own sign-in.
- Any other provider connects over IMAP and SMTP. The form checks the login against the mail host
  before it saves anything.
- Many mailboxes at once come from a CSV file.
- A connect link lets the person who owns a mailbox sign in on a page of ours, without an account in
  your workspace.

A mail host on a private, loopback or link-local address is refused.

## The stop-list

Addresses and domains your workspace never mails. A new server starts with the workspace's stop-list.

## Through the API and the MCP server

- **REST.** `https://app.gtm-api.com/email/v4`, documented in the
  [Email API reference](/api-reference/overview). The `Email-Box-SID` header names the server a call
  is for (`em_bx_...`, listed by `POST /api/email-boxes/search` on the ID and Teams API); a call
  without it goes to the workspace's primary server.
- **MCP.** Four mounts: `/mcp/email/accounts`, `/mcp/email/messaging`, `/mcp/email/deliverability` and
  `/mcp/email/infrastructure`. Every tool takes an optional `email_box_sid`. Buying, resizing and
  deleting servers are tools of `/mcp/id/billing`, behind the preview and confirm step.
- **Secrets stay out of the model.** Connecting a mailbox with its password and creating a sign-in
  application are REST only; an agent sends the mailbox's owner a connect link instead.
